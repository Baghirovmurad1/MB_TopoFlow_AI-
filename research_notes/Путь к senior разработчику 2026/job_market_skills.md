# Software Developer Job Market & In-Demand Skills 2025–2026: Junior vs. Senior in the Age of AI

_Research date: 26 Oct 2026. Data vintage is marked on each item. "Secondary" = the figure was reached only through an aggregator or news summary, not the primary report._

## 1. Junior hiring trends 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Through mid-2026 the entry-level squeeze has continued and appears to be widening. Employment of 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs is about 19% below trend (ADP data to June 2026). Software postings have recovered about 15% since early 2025, but that growth is concentrated in senior and AI-titled roles, and only about 4.5% of software postings are entry-level. Layoffs in 2026 had already passed the 2025 total by August.

### Cited Findings
**Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "Canaries in the Coal Mine" (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen)**
- First published in August 2025. A revised paper on 12 Aug 2026 uses ADP administrative payroll data covering millions of US workers through June 2026 — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)
- Employment of 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below where it would be if it had kept pace with less-exposed peers. Experienced workers show no comparable gap. The divergence "has widened steadily" since August 2025 — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)
- The gap comes mostly from reduced hiring of young workers, not from more separations. Adjustment shows up in employment, not in base pay — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)
- Declines are concentrated where AI substitutes for (automates) tasks. Where AI complements (augments) workers, employment is flat or rising, especially for experienced workers — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)
- Caveats from the authors:
  - They call the results "early, descriptive indicators" and not causal estimates.
  - The gap persists when excluding tech firms and when controlling for interest rates and remote work.
  - It weakens when controlling for education, some trends predate generative AI, and it is stronger in ADP data than in national survey benchmarks.
  - A 9 Feb 2026 update addressed the interest-rate and timing critiques.
  — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)
- Note: the revised-paper landing page reports results for AI-exposed occupations overall. It does not give a separate figure for software developers aged 22–25. The widely quoted figure of about a 20% decline for young developers since late 2022 comes from the August 2025 version and could not be re-verified here — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)

**Indeed Hiring Lab (US postings)**
- 8 Jul 2026 report:
  - US software-development postings are up "almost 15%" since 24 Feb 2025 (indexed to the Claude Code launch), while overall postings fell 7%.
  - Software postings are still about 27.5% below their Feb 2020 level, while overall postings are about flat versus 2020.
  — [Indeed Hiring Lab, 2026-07-08](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- Composition of the May 2025 to May 2026 net increase in software postings (the two categories overlap):
  - Senior roles accounted for 71%.
  - AI-titled jobs accounted for 37%.
  - Indeed says this is "not necessarily a broad-based recovery across all software roles."
  — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- Indeed treats the link to Claude Code and agentic AI as correlational. The evidence is "preliminary," and the rebound starts from a low base — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- In other large economies, the software share of postings is rising in most of six from Jan 2025 to Jun 2026. Germany and France are the exceptions — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- Jan 2026 update: jobs that mention AI are growing amid broader hiring weakness — [Indeed Hiring Lab, Jan 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/01/22/january-labor-market-update-jobs-mentioning-ai-are-growing-amid-broader-hiring-weakness/)
- Q1 2026 seniority mix of software postings (secondary, attributed to Indeed): about 4.5% entry-level vs about 69.3% senior. Over 47% of software postings mention AI, versus about 5% of all US postings — [Rockstar Developer University (secondary)](https://rockstardeveloperuniversity.com/entry-level-software-engineer-job-market-statistics/); [BlueLine Search (secondary, Aug 2026: ~26% below pre-pandemic)](https://bluelinesearch.ai/insights/software-dev-postings-26-below-prepandemic-indeed-august-2026)

  - Trackers use different scopes.

**Junior pay (US, Oct 2026)**
- ZipRecruiter: the average entry-level junior developer earns about $89k, with most between $67k and $87k — [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Entry-Level-Junior-Software-Developer)

### Inferences
- The market is bifurcated. It is not that there are "no jobs": demand has returned for experienced, AI-fluent engineers, while the bottom rung (pure implementation of well-specified tickets) is the most automated and the least hired.
- The "augment vs. automate" split in the Stanford data suggests a career strategy. A learner should aim for tasks AI complements (judgment, design, verification, security, domain ownership), not tasks it substitutes (boilerplate, simple CRUD, rote bug fixes).
- For a newcomer, the realistic path is to arrive with "junior+" evidence (deployed projects, production-like work) rather than competing for scarce pure-junior slots.

## 2. Most-valued skills (system design, debugging, review, testing, security, cloud, data, AI engineering)

### Takeaway
Survey data shows AI tools are near-universal, but trust in them is low. "Almost right" AI code is the top frustration, which raises the premium on verification skills: debugging, code review, testing and security. Python (+7 percentage points), Docker (+17 pp), PostgreSQL and cloud are the core stack. AI-titled roles and AI fluency drive the posting rebound.

### Cited Findings
**Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025: AI**
- Adoption:
  - 84% use or plan to use AI tools, up from 76%.
  - About 51% of professional developers use AI daily.
  - Favorable sentiment fell to about 60%, from over 70% in 2023–24.
  — [SO 2025 AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- Trust:
  - Only about 33% trust AI output, 46% distrust it, and about 3% "highly trust" it.
  - Experienced developers are the most skeptical, with about 20% "highly distrust."
  — [SO 2025 AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- Frustrations:
  - 66% cite "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite."
  - 45% say debugging AI-generated code takes more time.
  - 20% feel less confident in their own problem-solving.
  — [SO 2025 AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- Agents:
  - About 31% use AI agents at work at least monthly.
  - About 87% are concerned about agent accuracy, and about 81% about data security and privacy.
  — [SO 2025 AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- Early-career developers (1–5 years) use AI daily at 55.5%, versus 47% for experienced developers — [SO 2025 AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)

**Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025: technology**
- Python:
  - Used by 57.9% of all respondents and 54.8% of professionals.
  - Up 7 percentage points from 2024.
  - Used by 71.8% of those learning to code.
  — [SO 2025 Technology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)
- Docker: 71.1%, up 17 pp, the largest single-year increase of any technology — [SO 2025 Technology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)
- Cloud and orchestration: AWS 43.3%, Kubernetes 28.5%, Azure 26.3%, GCP 24.6% — [SO 2025 Technology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)
- Databases: PostgreSQL 55.6% (top), Redis 28% (+8%) — [SO 2025 Technology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)
- Models used for development: OpenAI GPT 81.4%, Claude Sonnet 42.8%, Gemini Flash 35.3% — [SO 2025 Technology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)

**JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2025** (24,534 developers, 194 countries, Apr–Jun 2025)
- 85% regularly use AI tools, and 62% rely on at least one AI coding assistant or agent — [JetBrains Research blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/); [InfoWorld via scour](https://scour.ing/p/https://www.infoworld.com/article/4077352/85-of-developers-use-ai-regularly-jetbrains-survey.html)
- 68% expect AI proficiency to become a job requirement — [JetBrains](https://devecosystem-2025.jetbrains.com/); [abp.io summary](https://abp.io/community/articles/jetbrains-state-of-developer-ecosystem-report-2025-key-z0638q5e)
- 23% worry about low-quality generated code — [abp.io summary](https://abp.io/community/articles/jetbrains-state-of-developer-ecosystem-report-2025-key-z0638q5e)
- The share saying AI saves 8+ hours a week rose from 9% to 19% — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/21/massive_jetbrains_dev_survey)
- TypeScript is on the rise — [heise](https://heise.de/-10771317)
- JetBrains acknowledges sample bias toward its own users — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/21/massive_jetbrains_dev_survey)

**Demand signals**
- Over 47% of software postings mention AI (secondary). AI-titled jobs made up 37% of the net posting increase from May 2025 to May 2026 — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- Levels.fyi 2025: AI/ML "is now core engineering," one of the largest and highest-paid SWE tracks — [Levels.fyi 2025 report](https://www.levels.fyi/2025/)
- Pragmatic Engineer 2026: "AI engineering: explosive demand." AI engineers get higher offers than SWEs, especially in equity — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/i/199358573/4-ai-engineering-explosive-demand)

### Inferences
- The scarce skills are the ones AI is worst at, in priority order:
  1. Verification: debugging, reading and reviewing code, writing tests and evals.
  2. System design and architecture trade-offs.
  3. Security and reliability.
  4. Problem framing and domain knowledge.
  
  The 66% "almost right" and 45% "debugging takes longer" statistics are direct evidence of this.
- Recommended core stack for a Python learner:
  - Python, SQL/PostgreSQL, Docker, one cloud (AWS is the most common), Git, CI.
  - Then LLM application engineering: RAG, tool-calling and agents, evals, observability, prompt-injection defenses.
  - Then MLOps and data-pipeline basics.

### Gaps

- I found no rigorous 2026 job-posting skill-frequency study covering RAG, agents and evals specifically. Lightcast or LinkedIn skills data was not retrieved.
- Stack Overflow 2026 or JetBrains 2026 survey results, if already published, were not found in this pass.

## 3. What defines senior / staff / architect level

### Takeaway
Seniority is defined by scope of impact and ambiguity handled, not by coding speed. Staff-plus roles split into distinct archetypes (Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand). In 2026, demand and pay increasingly concentrate at senior and staff levels, while management layers thin out.

### Cited Findings
- **Will Larson, StaffEng archetypes.** Staff-plus ladders "paper over several distinct roles hidden behind a single moniker":
  - **Tech Lead:** guides the approach and execution of one team or a small cluster. It is the most common archetype and often the first staff role.
  - **Architect:** owns the direction and quality of a critical technical domain (APIs, storage, cloud infra), works closely with the business and users, and is more common at large companies.
  - **Solver:** digs into arbitrarily complex, leadership-prioritized problems.
  - **Right Hand:** extends an executive's attention and scope, mostly in organizations with hundreds of engineers.
  - Being staff depends on role, behaviors, impact and organizational recognition, not on title alone.
  — [StaffEng – Archetypes](https://staffeng.com/guides/staff-archetypes/)
- **Pay by level (Levels.fyi End of Year 2025, US median total comp, 2025 data):**

  | Level | Median TC | Change vs 2024 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Entry | $155K | +1.6% |
  | Senior | $312K | +4.2% |
  | Staff | $457K | +7.5% |
  | Principal | $551K | −6.6% |

  Staff saw the fastest growth — [Levels.fyi 2025](https://www.levels.fyi/2025/)
- Pragmatic Engineer 2026: at the 80th percentile in the US, $300K+ base salary is now the norm for senior engineers. There are fewer engineering managers per engineer, and fewer VP and Director roles at Big Tech — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/i/201325168/3-mobile-and-frontend-demand-drops-ai-and-fde-surges)
- Indeed: 71% of the net software-posting increase from May 2025 to May 2026 was senior roles — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- Opinion (not data): engineers who know when to use AI, how to review AI-generated code, and how to direct AI at the right problems are significantly more valuable — [Addy Osmani, "The Next Two Years of Software Engineering"](https://addyosmani.com/blog/next-two-years/)
- Public ladder collections are available for direct study (Dropbox, GitLab, CircleCI and others) — [progression.fyi](https://progression.fyi/)
- Skill roadmaps (backend, Python, DevOps, AI engineer, software architect) — [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/)

### Inferences
- Applied to a learning plan, the ladder looks like this:
  - **Junior:** completes well-scoped tasks with guidance.
  - **Mid:** owns features end-to-end.
  - **Senior:** owns systems or projects, handles ambiguity, mentors, and makes design trade-offs.
  - **Staff/Architect:** sets direction across teams or domains, writes design docs and RFCs, and handles cross-team alignment and risk.
  
  This framing is consistent with Larson's archetypes, but the per-level details are a synthesis, not a quoted ladder.
- "Architect" in the Larson sense means domain ownership plus business alignment, not ivory-tower diagrams. That makes communication and written design docs core skills.
- AI raises the bar for juniors and raises the value of seniors. The pay data (Staff +7.5% vs Entry +1.6%) is consistent with that.

### Gaps
- Typical years of experience per level were not sourced in this pass. The common industry rule of thumb (senior about 5–8 years, staff about 8–12 or more) is not verified here and should not be quoted as fact.
- The contents of Tanya Reilly's "The Staff Engineer's Path" (O'Reilly, 2022) and Gergely Orosz's "The Software Engineer's Guidebook" (2023) were not fetched. Only their existence is known, and both predate the 2025–26 AI shift.
- Specific Dropbox, GitLab and CircleCI ladder text was not fetched (see progression.fyi).

## 4. Demand and salary: Python, AI engineers, remote/international (incl. CIS)

### Takeaway
AI engineers earn a clear premium. Senior remote pay for Eastern Europe runs about $80–120k, roughly 40–50% below US levels. I found no reliable Azerbaijan- or CIS-specific 2026 salary data. Remote contracts are possible, but sanctions and payment rails are a practical constraint (not researched here).

### Cited Findings
- Python is the most-used language among learners (71.8%) and is used by 54.8% of professionals, up 7 pp year over year — [SO 2025 Technology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)
- **AI engineer pay (US)**
  - All figures below are secondary aggregators citing Levels.fyi, Robert Half and PwC, unverified against the primaries.
  - Average total comp for AI-focused SWEs is about $245k (2025) — [Pin.com](https://pin.com/blog/ai-compensation-salary-guide)
  - Robert Half 2026 base salary for AI/ML engineers is $134k–$193k, with a $170.75k midpoint — [Interview Kickstart](https://www.interviewkickstart.com/articles/ai-engineer-salary)
  - Frontier-lab SWE median total comp is about $600k–$795k (May 2026) — [ThirstySprout](https://www.thirstysprout.com/post/ai-engineering-salary)
  - PwC 2025 AI Jobs Barometer reportedly found a 56% wage premium for AI skills — [Uvik](https://uvik.net/blog/ai-engineer-salary/)
- **Remote and international**
  - Lemon.io (updated Aug 2026): Eastern European senior developers earn about $80k–$120k a year, about 40–50% below US seniors. Europe holds 41% of its contracts (Ukraine, Poland, Romania lead) — [Lemon.io rate calculator](https://lemon.io/rate-calculator/)
  - Arc: the global average for remote developers is about $70,877 a year (self-reported) — [Arc.dev](https://arc.dev/remote-developer-salary)
  - Index.dev (Mar 2025, older data): Poland entry-level is about $36–45k and senior about $55–64k — [Index.dev](https://www.index.dev/blog/average-developer-salaries-eastern-europe)

  - Levels.fyi 2025: office-based roles grew 12% in its data volume, a sign of return-to-office pressure — [Levels.fyi 2025](https://www.levels.fyi/2025/)

### Inferences
- For someone in Azerbaijan or Russia:
  - Senior remote B2B/contract work for EU, US or Middle East clients is the main path to high pay.
  - Senior-level skill plus strong written English and async communication matter more for remote work than for local roles, because juniors are rarely hired remotely.
- Python plus LLM application engineering is the highest-leverage niche for remote work. AI-titled roles drive posting growth, and Python dominates AI tooling.

## 5. Leveraging a cybersecurity background (AppSec, DevSecOps, secure AI)

### Takeaway
Security is skill-constrained rather than headcount-constrained. The top needed skills are AI (41%), cloud security (36%) and application security (28%). That overlaps exactly with a Python, cloud and LLM-app learning path, so "secure AI / AppSec engineer" is a strong differentiator.

### Cited Findings
- **ISC2 2025 Cybersecurity Workforce Study** (more than 16,000 respondents)
  - Findings via secondary coverage; the primary report was not opened.
  - 59% report critical or significant skills shortages, up from 44% in 2024.
  - For the first time, ISC2 did not publish a global headcount-gap estimate, because skills shortages matter more than headcount.
  — [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/skills-shortages-headcount-2025/); [SecurityInfoWatch](https://www.securityinfowatch.com/cybersecurity/article/55338497/cybersecurity-skills-gaps-now-outpace-headcount-shortages-isc2-workforce-study-finds)
- Most-needed security skills (ISC2 2025):

  | Skill | Share of respondents |
  |---|---|
  | AI | 41% |
  | Cloud security | 36% |
  | Risk assessment | 29% |
  | Application security | 28% |
  | Security engineering | 27% |
  | GRC | 27% |

  — [MeriTalk](https://meritalk.com/articles/isc2-report-cyber-experts-say-they-need-skills-more-than-headcount/)
- Budget pressure:
  - 36% report budget cuts and 24% report layoffs, flat versus 2024.
  - Nearly half are self-teaching AI skills.
  — [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/skills-shortages-headcount-2025/)
- ISC2 India subset (Sep 2026): AI 52%, cloud security 43%, AppSec 38% — [ISC2 Insights 2026-09](https://www.isc2.org/insights/2026/09/research-india-cybersecurity-workforce-reaches-a-turning-point-skills-ai)
- Developer concerns:
  - 81% of developers worry about data security and privacy with AI agents.
  - 87% worry about agent accuracy.
  — [SO 2025 AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)

### Inferences
- A security background can feed into several paths:
  - **AppSec / product security:** secure code review, threat modeling, SAST/DAST in CI.
  - **DevSecOps / cloud security:** IaC scanning, IAM, Kubernetes security.
  - **AI security:** prompt injection, agent tool-permissioning, data leakage, LLM red-teaming, OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications.
- The 81% concern about AI-agent security points to underserved demand at the intersection of AI and security.
- Security habits (threat modeling, least privilege) are also senior/architect competencies, so they double-count on the ladder.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 posting or salary data specifically for AppSec, DevSecOps or AI-security engineer roles.
- The OWASP LLM Top 10 and specific AI-security role definitions were not fetched in this pass.

## 6. Role of portfolio, open source and certifications in hiring (2026)

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous 2026 recruiter survey. Available (mostly vendor or opinion) sources agree that deployed projects and portfolios are the primary signal, with certifications secondary and most useful for cloud and ATS filtering. Background and pedigree matter more as junior slots shrink.

### Cited Findings
- Pragmatic Engineer, Jun 2026: with fewer intern and new-grad slots, candidates' work and educational backgrounds "matter more than ever" — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/i/201325168/3-mobile-and-frontend-demand-drops-ai-and-fde-surges)
- Opinion and vendor sources:
  - Degrees and certifications act as HR checkboxes. Portfolios, deployed projects and interview performance close offers. Employers look at commit messages, READMEs and deployed projects — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/placemeright/the-real-value-of-github-projects-in-todays-hiring-market-placemeright-1lj8); [SimeonOnSecurity](https://simeononsecurity.com/software-development-career-playbook/education-and-certifications/do-i-need-a-degree-to-become-a-developer/)
  - Cloud certifications (AWS, Azure, GCP) get the most credit. CompTIA certifications help pass ATS screens for IT-adjacent roles — [Proxify](https://proxify.io/knowledge-base/developer-types/how-do-certifications-impact-a-developers-job-prospects)
  - AI-assisted exam prep has reduced the signal value of some certifications — [mages.edu.sg](https://mages.edu.sg/blog/do-you-really-need-a-certification-for-a-web-dev-course-in-2026/)
- Older view (2023, pre-shift): recruiters and executives favored certifications — [IEEE Computer Society](https://www.computer.org/publications/tech-news/build-your-career/computer-science-degree)

### Inferences
- When AI can generate toy projects, a credible 2026 portfolio is one that shows judgment AI can't fake:
  - Deployed systems with tests, CI, observability and a security review.
  - Design docs and ADRs.
  - An LLM application with an eval suite and documented failure modes.
  - Merged pull requests to real open-source projects, which provide external code-review evidence.
- Certifications fit as targeted ATS and cloud signals. Examples: AWS associate-level, and security certifications if pursuing an AppSec or cloud-security track. They are not a substitute for shipped work.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative 2025–2026 data (e.g., a LinkedIn, Hired or HackerRank survey) on how recruiters weight GitHub, open source or certifications. All 2026 claims here come from vendor or opinion sources.
- Hired's reports were not found. Hired reportedly wound down, but that is not verified here.
